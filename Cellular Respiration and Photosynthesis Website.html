Cellular Respiration and Photosynthesis

Cellular Respiration

What is cellular respiration?

Cellular respiration is the process in which animals can make ATP. Cellular respiration happens in the mitochondria. The reactants of cellular respiration are glucose and oxygen. The products are carbon dioxide (CO2), water (H2O), and ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate).

What are the steps of cellular respiration?

There are three steps to cellular respiration, Glycolysis, the Krebs Cycle, and the Electron Transport Chain

Glycolysis

  • It happens in the cytoplasm of the mitochondria.
  • Glucose is the reactant that is divided into two pyruvate molecules.
  • The two pyruvate go to the matrix of the mitochondria for the Krebs Cycle.
  • There is a net gain of 2 ATP.

The Krebs Cycle

  • The Krebs Cycle happens in the matrix the "curvy lines" in the mitochondria.
  • It goes around twice.
  • The byproducts are carbon dioxide and two ATP molecules.
  • The Krebs cycle also produces FADH2 and NADH, but they are used in the ETC.

Electron Transport Chain(ETC)

  • The ETC happens in the intermembrane space of the mitochondria.
  • The reactant of the ETC is oxygen.
  • The byproduct of the ETC is water.
  • In the end, cellular respiration produces a grand total of 32 ATP

Photosynthesis

What is photosynthesis?

Photosynthesis is the process of how plants make food. It involves the absorbtion of sunlight and water. It also uses Carbon Dioxide to make glucose and oxygen. The steps of photosynthesis include the Light Dependent Reaction, and the Light Independent Reaction.

The Light Dependent Reaction

  • The reactant is water(H2O) and sunlight.
  • It occurs in the thylakoids.
  • The product is oxygen.

Light Independent Reaction

  • It's also called a dark reaction.
  • The reactant is carbon dioxide.
  • It happens in the stroma.
  • The product is glucose.
